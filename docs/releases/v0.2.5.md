New: the mouse wheel changes the radio station

- While you drive, the mouse wheel now changes the radio station: down is the next station, up the one before (#90). Before, the wheel did nothing in a vehicle, and the game itself can only step forwards.
- It's part of the game's settings (F2) → Keyboard: **Mouse wheel**, which is on by default. If you've bound the wheel to a control, it keeps doing only that.
- While the radio plays an announcement the game doesn't let you change the station, with the wheel or any other way.

Purple Haze

- Purple Haze: above 30 fps the game stayed on "Loading..." for ever after the truck rammed your van (#41). At 60 fps and up the truck didn't stop behind the van as it does at 30. It rolled on and ran over one of the two men just as he appeared behind the black screen, and the game then waited for ever for a line he could no longer say. The mission now plays through at any frame rate.
- The same kind of wait can no longer hang any other mission: if someone dies before their line has loaded, the game carries on without it.
- Purple Haze: on a widescreen picture the haze only covered the middle and left the sides clear, and it pulled the clock and the weapon icon in from the edge of the screen (#93). It now covers the whole picture.

Fixes

- On foot, turning the camera toward a nearby building with the mouse made it stop dead at the edge of the wall, and it stayed stuck until you turned back. It now keeps turning and moves in closer to Vic, as it always did with a gamepad.
- Memory: the longer you played and the more of the city you drove through, the more memory the game used, and it never gave any back. Here it grew by up to 2 GB an hour, and one player's PC with 8 GB showed the game at 5.3 GB (#115). The game kept a copy of everything it had ever drawn. It now keeps only what it used recently, so its memory levels off: on the same test drive it settles between 600 and 700 MB and is still there after 40 minutes. Before, it was at 840 MB after 15 minutes and still climbing.
- Helicopters: at high frame rates the rotor blades did their damage far too fast when they touched a wall or anything else. At 144 fps a helicopter was on fire in under a second (#102). The blades now do the same damage per second as on the PS2 at any frame rate.
- Recording with Bandicam, or taking a Steam screenshot, filled the console with hundreds of lines and wrote picture files next to the game. F12 was also a developer key in the game (#89). The game now leaves F12 alone.
- Laptops with two graphics cards: the game could start on the slow integrated one (#112). It now picks the dedicated card. New in the game's settings (F2) → Graphics: **Graphics card** lists the cards your PC has by name, the fastest first, with "(integrated)" after the one built into the processor. The first is the default. This couldn't be tried on a laptop with two cards here, so it's hoped to fix the problem. The change applies after a restart. On Linux the game already picked the dedicated card; the setting is there too.

Also

- Windows: vcs.exe now has an icon, and its version and description show in Explorer (#91).

Updating: on Windows, go to Settings → Updates → Update now in the launcher, close the launcher and start it again, then update the build. The build only compiles part of the game again and links it, so it's much quicker than a first build. Your saves aren't touched. You can also extract the zip into a new folder. On Linux, update from Settings in the same way, or download the new AppImage.
